# Mural 0.4.0

Mural now runs on iPhone and Android, every word you keep becomes a flash card, and any word Mural
shows you can be looked up with a tap.

## Mural on your phone

### iPhone

Mural runs on the iPhone as the same app as on the computer: Talk, Shadow, Dialog and Words, all
eight languages, and the on-device voice for Shadow.

- **Installing.** Mural isn't on the App Store, so iOS runs it only when it is signed for your own
  Apple ID. A free Apple ID is enough.
  - With a Mac and Xcode, [docs/ios-iphone.md](https://github.com/rustsky/mural-dioxus/blob/v0.4.0/docs/ios-iphone.md)
    walks through it step by step, and `scripts/install-ios-device.sh` then builds, signs and
    installs Mural on the connected iPhone in one go.
  - Without Xcode, download `Mural-0.4.0-ios-unsigned.ipa` from this release and install it with
    [Sideloadly](https://sideloadly.io) or [AltStore](https://altstore.io), which sign it with your
    Apple ID.
  - Either way, turn on **Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode** on the phone. With a free
    Apple ID the signature lasts 7 days; install again to renew it, and your learning stays.
- **Your API key is kept in the iPhone's keychain**, as on the Mac.
- **Pinyin and language detection use the iPhone's own Apple frameworks**, the same as on the Mac.
- **Made for iOS 27**, which only opens apps that use its scene-based life cycle. Mural includes a
  newer version of the window library it is built on (tao 0.37, in `vendor/tao`) to meet that.

### Android

- **Installing.** Download `Mural-0.4.0-arm64-release.apk` and open it on the phone. It is signed
  with the standard debug key, so Android asks you to allow installs from outside the Play Store.
- **Your API key is kept in a file only Mural can read**, in its private folder. Android has no
  keychain Mural can use yet.
- Mural asks for the microphone the first time you speak.

### Not on phones yet

- **Backups.** Exporting and importing a backup needs the system's file picker, which Mural doesn't
  use yet on iPhone or Android, so the buttons are hidden there.
- **Phones are new.** Mural runs on an iPhone and has been tried in the iOS Simulator, but it hasn't
  been tested on many phones yet; tell us what doesn't work on yours.

## Flash cards

Every word on your Words list is now a flash card.

- **A review shows the word, then its meaning and an example.** Answer **Again**, **Hard**,
  **Good** or **Easy**; each button shows when the card will come back. On a keyboard, Space shows
  the answer and 1–4 picks one, as in Anki.
- **Scheduling follows Anki's essentials.** New cards go through 1- and 10-minute steps, then
  graduate to a day. After that the gap grows with each review, faster for cards you find easy and
  slower for hard ones. A card you forget starts over, and a late review still counts. At most 20
  new cards come each day.
- **The Words tab shows what is due, new and learning**, and each word shows where its card stands.
- **Add a word by hand** with *Add a word*.

Flash cards measure whether you recognise a word. The bars next to each word still measure
something else: whether you use it yourself in conversation.

Card schedules are kept on your device, like Shadow progress. *Delete all conversations and
learning* in Settings removes them too.

## Look up any word

Tap any word in the language you are learning, wherever it appears: what Mural says in Talk and what
you said, the opening greeting, a Dialog scenario's title, goal and tasks, and the Shadow phrase.

The card shows the word's dictionary form, its meaning, and at most one short line on how it is
used, instead of the paragraphs some models used to write. *Keep* adds it to your words straight
from that answer.

## Fixes

- **The voice download no longer gets stuck.** If you left a Shadow drill while its voice was
  downloading and came back, a second download started over the first. It could then stay on
  *Unpacking the voice…* until Mural was restarted. Downloads now happen one at a time, and a drill
  opened mid-download shows the progress of the one already running. This affected every platform.
- **Dialog's first scenario explains its tasks correctly.** The breakfast or bakery scenario said
  "rolls" in the English hints for Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese, where the task asks for
  bread, and for Chinese, where it asks for steamed buns.
- **Sheets fit small screens.** Settings and the other sheets could be taller than the screen on a
  phone, which pushed their title under the status bar.
- **The second step of onboarding fits a phone screen**, instead of hiding its last line behind the
  consent text.
